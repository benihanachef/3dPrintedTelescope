Hardware:
-3x metal rod or pipe; 0.5" OD, 3' length.  Aluminum or steel.
-50x #10-24 machine screws, 7/16 or 1/2" length
-8x #10-24 machine screws, 3/4" length
-2x #10-24 machine screw, THUMB HEAD.  Optionally, all your screws can be thumb head, and there's a few places where you'd want one.
-10x #10-24 machine screw, 2".  Ideally, socket head or thumb.

-100x #10-24 hex nut
-4x spring (needs to be moderately stiff, and to slide over a #10-24 screw without issues.  Otherwise any spring should work)
-114mm/900mm spherical mirror set.  These can be found in huge quantities online.
-silicone glue

-Eyepieces.  I recommend SVBONY, on amazon/ebay/etc.  A "23mm aspheric" (you might need to click the assortment and specify just the 23mm) ($10-15) or "25mm plossl" -- and a "6mm goldline" ($30-35).  These are low and high power, respectively, and you want to be able to do both.  Low power is great for the moon, and larger deep sky objects such as nebulae and galaxies.  High power is important for planets in rich detail -- this telescope is powerful enough to observe the rings of saturn or storms of jupiter.

Optional:
-3x #10-24 knurled nut (these can also be printed)
-Flat black spraypaint/masking tools
-teflon tape


An 0.6mm nozzle is recommended for a blend of print speed, large layer height, but some modicum of resolution.


PRINT DIRECTIONS
NO SUPPORTS NEEDED ANYWHERE
Your printer needs to be reliable enough for large prints up to 8 hours long (longer with an .4 nozzle).  Beyond that- these prints shouldn't be challenging.  They are very conscientiously designed for FDM, with simple topology and small bed footprints.

Most parts should have three perimeters with an 0.4mm nozzle (2 at 0.6mm) - and be printed with thick layers, optionally with adaptive layer height.  My go-to was 30% cubic infill, and I don't believe I deviated from that anywhere except for the Z-bearings which were 50%, and the secondary holder which is solid.  If you use heavy eyepieces, you might print the focuser solid.


Focuser: Both of these need thin layers for the threads to print nicely.  For now, I recommend 0.2mm for the outer focuser (FocuserO) and 0.25mm for the inner focuser (FocuserI).  I recommend printing thin slices of this as a test, just to be sure your printer's calibration and the print settings are adequate for the threads to interface nicely.


Spider: Choose one.  Google "diffraction spikes vs spider" to see the effect these can have.  You will need to pause this print twice -- your slicer should have settings which allow this to be automated.  It should be paused at the top of the two cavities - you will need to insert a #10-24 nut.

Choose whether you want planets on the Z-bearing interface.
